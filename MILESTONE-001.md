# Milestone 001 — a superficial change to the house

**Opened:** 2026-07-29
**Target:** end of week (2026-08-02 assumed — confirm)
**Status:** open

## The claim

Zach and Chris agree on a superficial, aesthetic modification to the house, **and the
change is made.** Both halves are required. Agreement without execution is not the
milestone; execution without prior agreement is not either.

This is the core unit of the game — the smallest thing that is genuinely a change in
the world rather than a change in a document.

## Why this and not something larger

Per `POSTAL.md` §III, the Chris–Paul exchange failed for want of evidence from outside
itself. Milestone 001 is deliberately trivial in ambition and non-trivial in kind: its
value is not the change but the *information* produced by having actually made one.

Long-term vision remains **install something in a gallery** (`POSTAL.md` §VI). Nobody
needs to work out how 001 leads there yet. Working out the details is the work.

## Deadline as instrument

End of week is a real deadline and a measuring device. **If the modification does not
happen, that is data** — arguably better data than success. Either outcome triggers
reevaluation, not just the good one.

## Variables — DRAFT, needs both principals

Named now so the milestone produces information rather than just an outcome. None of
these are settled.

| Variable | Question | Proposed |
|---|---|---|
| Which house | "the house" — literally Zach's? | Zach's, per the conversation. Confirm. |
| Superficial | What bounds it? | Reversible; nothing structural or permanent; trivial cost |
| Agreed | What counts as consent? | Both principals explicit, in writing, in this repo |
| Who executes | Agents negotiate; who acts? | Humans. Agents cannot modify a house. |
| Evidence | What records it? | A photograph, committed here |
| Deletion | What does this retire? | Per §V — decided at integration, not now |

## Hypothesis

That a single real, agreed, executed change produces information the text could not
generate from inside itself — most likely about the *cost of agreeing*, not about
houses. The Chris–Paul exchange never tested whether consensus survives contact with
execution. This does.

Secondary: that "superficial" will prove harder to define than to do, and that the
negotiation over what counts will be more informative than the change.
